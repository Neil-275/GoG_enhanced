For every step, you must first generate a `Thought` that decomposes the current state of the question, followed immediately by exactly one `Action`.

Your goal is to answer the question using entity IDs from the KG.

## Actions

### Propose[Current entity | Query]

Examines one entity in the KG. It returns:

* `Local context` — the relation types recorded around that entity, split into
  outgoing and incoming. This shows you the shape of the graph around the
  entity. It lists relation names only, not entities.
* For each relation it judges relevant to your Query, labelled groups:
  * `(observed, outgoing)` — recorded triples where the entity is the subject.
  * `(observed, incoming)` — recorded triples where the entity is the object.
    Both directions are always reported when they exist, so a relation may
    appear twice under one `Selected:` heading. A direction with nothing new to
    show is omitted; when neither direction has anything new, a single
    `(observed): No new edges retrieved` line appears instead.
  * `(predicted, unverified)` — triples proposed by a link predictor. These are
    not recorded in the KG. They are informed guesses about edges that may be
    missing.

  Entities are listed in brackets on the side of the triple they occupy, so
  `262, son_of, [264, 265]` means both 264 and 265 are sons of 262, while
  `[264, 265], son_of, 262` means 262 is a son of both. Read the bracket
  position to know which side the entities are on.

  The same entity id may appear under both an observed and a predicted group
  for one relation. That is not a duplicate: it means one direction of that
  edge is recorded and the opposite direction is only a guess.

  Only edges you have not already been shown are reported. Proposing again on
  the same entity and relation surfaces further edges, not the same ones.

Rules:

* The first action must be `Propose` on the topic entity.
* Propose takes exactly one entity. Do not pass several entities at once.
* The Query must clearly state what information you want about that entity.
* Do not repeat the same Propose with the same arguments.

### Collect[Answer_1 | Answer_2 | ...]

Collects entity IDs that are likely answers.

Rules:

* Collect immediately when an observed triple directly matches the question.
* Collect when a path of evidence strongly supports the answer.
* Do not collect entities merely because they are connected to the topic entity; the relation or path must support the question.
* After collecting, continue exploring other relevant branches if they may reveal more answers.

### Finish

Stops the search.

Rule:

* Finish only when useful evidence has been exhausted or no relevant direction remains.

## Choosing the next entity

Each Propose gives you new entity IDs. The next entity you Propose on may be
**any entity ID that has appeared anywhere earlier in this trajectory** — the
topic entity, an entity from any previous Observation, or an entity you have
already collected. You are not restricted to the most recent Observation. If an
entity from an earlier step is the better lead, go back to it.

Observed entities are stronger evidence for continuing exploration than
predicted ones, because observed triples are recorded in the KG while predicted
triples are not. Prefer to continue through an observed entity when one fits.
Predicted entities are still usable when they match the question better than any
observed entity — treat them as leads to be corroborated rather than as
established facts.

## Important Context

* Each triple has the form `(subject, relation, object)`.
* The relation is directed from subject to object. `X son_of Y` means X is the son of Y.
* Entity labels are masked; reason from relations and graph structure only.
* Do not try to identify real-world labels.
* Do not repeat the same action with the same arguments.

## Evidence Policy

1. Direct observed evidence
   If an observed triple directly matches the question, collect the answer entities immediately.

2. Strong indirect evidence
   If observed triples form a strong reasoning path to an answer, collect the answer entities.

3. Predicted-only evidence
   A predicted triple alone is a lead, not an answer. Prefer to corroborate it —
   by Proposing on the predicted entity, or on another entity that would share a
   relation with it — before collecting. If corroboration is not available and
   the prediction is the only thing that fits the question, it may still be
   collected.

4. No supported answer yet
   Continue only through entities whose relations may produce evidence relevant to the question.

## Example 1:

Question: Who is 262's child?
Topic Entity: [262]

Thought 1: I need to find the children of 262. A child of 262 would be recorded as someone who is a son or daughter of 262, so I should look at the relations around 262 that connect it to its children.
Action 1: Propose[262 | who are the children of 262]

Observation 1:
Local context for 262:
  outgoing: [son_of, father_of, uncle_of, nephew_of]
  incoming: [son_of, wife_of, sister_of, aunt_of, uncle_of]

Selected: son_of
  (observed, outgoing):        262, son_of, [132, 134]
  (observed, incoming):        [264, 265, 266], son_of, 262
  (predicted, unverified):     [263, 132], son_of, 262

Selected: father_of
  (observed, outgoing):        262, father_of, [266]
  (predicted, unverified):     262, father_of, [265, 264]

Thought 2: The son_of groups point in opposite directions and only one of them answers the question. The outgoing group says 262 is a son of 132 and 134 — those are 262's own parents, not its children, so I ignore them. The incoming group says 264, 265 and 266 are each a son of 262, which is what I asked for. Among the predictions, 132 is already recorded as someone 262 is a son of, so predicting it as a child of 262 as well would make 132 both parent and child — I discard it. The father_of predictions point at 264 and 265, which is consistent with the observed son_of edges, but those edges are already sufficient. I should collect all three.
Action 2: Collect[264 | 265 | 266]

Observation 2:
Collected the answers: [264 | 265 | 266]

Thought 3: The children of 262 are accounted for by the observed son_of edges. The remaining relations around 262 concern its own parents and siblings and do not identify further children.
Action 3: Finish

## Example 2:

Question: Who is 2083's aunt?
Topic Entity: [2083]

Thought 1: I need to find the aunt of 2083. An aunt would be recorded as someone who is aunt_of 2083. If that edge is not recorded, I can instead reach an aunt through 2083's parents, since an aunt is a sister of a parent. I should look at 2083 and ask for both.
Action 1: Propose[2083 | who is the aunt or the parent of 2083]

Observation 1:
Local context for 2083:
  outgoing: [son_of, nephew_of, brother_of]
  incoming: [father_of, mother_of, sister_of, uncle_of, brother_of]

Selected: aunt_of
  (observed):                  No new edges retrieved
  (predicted, unverified):     [2079, 1465, 1333], aunt_of, 2083

Selected: father_of
  (observed, incoming):        [1335], father_of, 2083
  (predicted, unverified):     [1329], father_of, 2083

Selected: sister_of
  (observed, incoming):        [2086, 2087], sister_of, 2083
  (predicted, unverified):     [2081], sister_of, 2083

Thought 2: No aunt_of edge is recorded for 2083, so the aunt is not directly observable. The predictor proposes 2079, 1465 and 1333, but these are unverified and I should not collect on that alone. There is a strong observed lead: 1335 is recorded as the father of 2083. An aunt of 2083 would be a sister of 1335, so I can test the predictions by examining 1335's sisters.
Action 2: Propose[1335 | who are the sisters of 1335]

Observation 2:
Local context for 1335:
  outgoing: [father_of, son_of, brother_of, husband_of, nephew_of]
  incoming: [sister_of, daughter_of, son_of, wife_of, aunt_of]

Selected: sister_of
  (observed, incoming):        [1465, 2079], sister_of, 1335
  (predicted, unverified):     [1331], sister_of, 1335

Selected: brother_of
  (observed, outgoing):        1335, brother_of, [1333, 1334]
  (observed, incoming):        [1333], brother_of, 1335
  (predicted, unverified):     1335, brother_of, [1329]

Thought 3: 2079 is observed as a sister of 1335, and 1335 is observed as the father of 2083. A sister of a parent is an aunt, so 2079 is an aunt of 2083. This corroborates the earlier prediction with observed edges. Note that 1333 was also predicted as an aunt of 2083, but the brother_of groups record 1333 as a brother of 1335 in both directions, so 1333 is an uncle rather than an aunt and should not be collected.
Action 3: Collect[2079]

Observation 3:
Collected the answer: [2079]

Thought 4: I can strengthen this using an entity from Observation 1 rather than the most recent one. 2087 was observed there as a sister of 2083, so 2087 shares both parents with 2083 and must share the same aunts. If 2079 is recorded as the aunt of 2087, that confirms the conclusion independently.
Action 4: Propose[2087 | who is the aunt of 2087]

Observation 4:
Local context for 2087:
  outgoing: [daughter_of, sister_of, niece_of, wife_of]
  incoming: [aunt_of, father_of, mother_of, sister_of, uncle_of]

Selected: aunt_of
  (observed, incoming):        [2079], aunt_of, 2087
  (predicted, unverified):     [1333], aunt_of, 2087

Thought 5: 2079 is directly observed as the aunt of 2087, and 2087 is a sister of 2083, so 2079 is confirmed as an aunt of 2083. That answer is already collected, and the other predicted candidates were ruled out. No relevant direction remains.
Action 5: Finish
